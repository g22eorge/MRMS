04

Corrections to my own findings

Six findings across both passes were wrong. All six shared a cause — a measurement that looked precise and measured the wrong thing. Worth recording, because the same traps will catch the next person:

  • "Returns have no module." They do — via credit notes, with a stock leg that only fires when goods physically come back. Correctly designed; I had searched for a model named Return and stopped.
  • "Two tax conventions in lineTotal." My invariant ignored tax. With lines + tax, production is zero.
  • "Mobile nav clips the last row." I measured the nav overlapping itself.
  • "Restricted pages return an empty shell." They redirect correctly in a browser; curl can't follow what the RSC payload does.
  • "Unknown ids render a proper not-found." This one I got right first, then wrongly corrected — I had grepped raw HTML, which counts the component sitting in the script bundle. It really was a blank page, and is now genuinely fixed.
  • "No customer account statement." There is one, and it works: a statement section on the client page with a running-balance table, a PDF, and send-by-WhatsApp-or-email. I checked before building and found the feature I was about to duplicate.

The rule that came out of it: curl is the wrong instrument for anything React renders, and a grep over raw HTML counts things that never reach the screen. Every UI claim in this second pass was verified in a browser, with scripts stripped.

DONEDocuments
Five invoice-numbering formats coexist
What it was
Filed as legacy residue and cosmetic. It was neither. Invoice numbers raised from a job were never allocated from the document sequence — they were derived from the job number by deriveDocNumberFromJob, which returns EIS/INV/2026/0044 for a slash-era job but falls back to INV- + the raw job number for anything else. Job numbering has changed shape four times, so the invoice book inherited four shapes. The one entry that looked like an anomaly, INV-EI-2026-0030 issued six days after the switch, was simply a job received on 15 July invoiced late — not a regression.
Why it was still spreading
A second path made it self-sustaining: the render-and-send branch (WhatsApp send, the PDF queue, a plain download) stamped a derived number onto Job.invoiceNumber with no allocation and no Invoice row. The persist path then faithfully reused that number as the invoice's own. 30 jobs were still holding dash-era numbers — 9 in care, 21 in commercial — and each would have minted another off-format invoice on its next send. Commercial's TFX-001 would have produced INV-TFX-001, a fifth shape, in a paying tenant's book.
Fixed
Only a number a job has already been issued is reused; a derived number is never handed to the allocator. Everything unnumbered now takes the next EIS/INV/YYYY/NNNN from DocumentSequence, which is what standalone invoices always did. The render-and-send branch allocates properly before stamping.
Not changed
No issued invoice was renumbered. 71 jobs keep the numbers already printed on documents customers hold — renumbering those would break reconciliation to fix an appearance.
DONEDocuments
Quotation numbers were never stored anywhere
What it was
Found while fixing the invoice numbering above, and worse in one respect: a quotation raised from a job prints a number but never creates a Quotation row. The number existed nowhere in the database — it was rebuilt from the job number on every render, and survived only inside audit-log JSON. It inherited the job-number format the same way invoices did: care's quoted jobs carry two shapes, QT-EI-2026-0007 and EIS-6/QT/2026/0016.
Knock-on effect
The invoice PDF re-derived the quotation number for its own cross-reference, so an invoice could cite a quote number that had never appeared on any quote the customer received.
Fixed
Job.quotationNumber now mirrors Job.invoiceNumber: allocated once from DocumentSequence, stored, and reused by every later render, with a re-read inside the transaction so two racing sends cannot each allocate. The invoice prints the stored number. Because job quotation numbers share the QT counter without being Quotation rows, the uniqueness check and the sequence seed scan now read the job column too — otherwise a reseed could reissue a number already sent.
Not changed
Every already-quoted job was backfilled with the number it has been sending — 18 in care, 0 in commercial, no collisions — so no quote a customer holds was renumbered. Only quotes issued from here on take an allocated EIS/QT/YYYY/NNNN.

What I would do next

1. Record BETTER_AUTH_SECRET somewhere outside Vercel. The cheapest item on this page and the only one that is irreversible if left. Everything else can be fixed after the fact; this one cannot be recovered at all.

2. Decide the commercial messaging story. Seven paying tenants currently cannot reach their customers. Whether they share Eagle Info's channels or get their own is a product decision, not a configuration one — which is why it is still open.

3. Paste in the Meta app secret to finish inbound WhatsApp on care — the last piece of a feature that is otherwise complete and already sending outbound.

The financial core, tenant isolation and the repair chain are the parts that are hardest to build and most expensive to get wrong. All three now hold under testing that tried to break them, on production data as well as local.
